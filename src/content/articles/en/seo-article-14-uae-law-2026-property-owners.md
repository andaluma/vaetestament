In June 2026 a new Civil Transactions Law came into force in the United Arab Emirates: **Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025**. To most people that sounds like a technical change. For foreign owners of UAE property, the law contains one provision you should know about.

## What does the new law say?

The law provides that UAE assets of a foreign national who dies **without a will and without identifiable heirs** go to a charitable endowment, a **Waqf**.

In other words: if there is no will and no heirs can be identified, your family and friends do not decide what happens to your apartment. The property goes to a fund.

## Who is most at risk?

- **Single owners without a partner or children.** Here the risk is direct. If you want to leave your apartment to a sibling, a niece or a close friend, that has to be in a will.
- **Unmarried partners.** An unmarried partner is not a legal heir in the UAE. Without a will, your partner does not automatically inherit your UAE property.
- **Married couples with children.** The direct Waqf risk is limited, because there are identifiable heirs. The other problem remains: without a will, your heirs have to go through a procedure at a UAE court.

## What does not change?

The law does not change the main point: **a will from your home country is in most cases not enough for UAE property.** Without a will registered in the UAE, it is not certain how your property will be distributed. Which law applies depends on your residence, nationality and the court's assessment.

It also remains the case that heirs without a will must first go through a UAE court procedure. That often takes 12 months or longer and typically costs EUR 10,000 or more, including legalisation, sworn translations and local lawyers. [Read what happens without a will](/articles/dubai-property-without-will/).

## How do you prevent this?

With a **DIFC will**, as a non-Muslim you decide in advance who inherits your UAE property. There is then a will, so the Waqf rule does not apply, and your heirs do not need a full inheritance procedure.

For property in your personal name, a **DIFC Property Will** is almost always enough. You register it fully remotely; the whole process usually takes 2 to 8 weeks.
