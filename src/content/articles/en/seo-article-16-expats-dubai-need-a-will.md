Many expats in Dubai own their apartment or villa, and assume the will they made back home covers it. For property in the UAE, that is usually not the case.

## Why a home-country will is usually not enough

A will drafted in Europe, North America or elsewhere is generally not recognised in the UAE for property located there. Without a will registered in the UAE, it is not certain how your property will be distributed. Which law applies depends on your residence, nationality and the court's assessment, and your heirs first have to go through a procedure at a UAE court. That often takes 12 months or longer and typically costs EUR 10,000 or more.

Since June 2026 there is an additional risk under the new Civil Transactions Law: UAE assets of a foreign national without a will and without identifiable heirs go to a charitable endowment (Waqf). [Read more about the new law](/articles/uae-law-2026-property-owners/).

## Living in the UAE does not change that

Being a resident, even with a Golden Visa, does not make your home-country will valid for UAE property. A **DIFC will** is open to all non-Muslims who own property in the UAE, residents and non-residents alike. As a resident you can use your Emirates ID in the registration, but it is not required.

## Which DIFC will fits an expat?

- **Property Will:** for property in your personal name (up to 5 properties) in any of the seven Emirates. This is enough for most owners.
- **Full Will:** also covers bank accounts, investments and vehicles in the UAE, property held through a company, and **guardianship of minor children**. Many expat families with young children in Dubai need this one.

[Property Will or Full Will: which one do you need?](/articles/property-will-or-full-will/)

## Couples

Married couples can register **mirror wills**: two separate wills in one appointment, often leaving everything to each other with the children as substitute beneficiaries. Unmarried partners are not legal heirs in the UAE, so for them a will is even more important; each partner registers their own will.

## How it works

Registration is done online through the DIFC portal, and the signing appointment takes place by video with two witnesses (18 or over, not a beneficiary or a beneficiary's partner). The whole process usually takes 2 to 8 weeks.
