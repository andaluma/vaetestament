In your DIFC will you decide not only **who** inherits your UAE property, but also **who makes sure** that happens: the executor. Many owners fill this in quickly, yet it matters a great deal after your death.

## What does an executor do?

The executor carries out your will after your death. In practice that includes:

- submitting the will to the DIFC court;
- dealing with the Dubai Land Department;
- settling any debts or costs;
- transferring the property to the beneficiaries.

After death, the DIFC court issues the Grant of Probate, in simple cases usually within a few weeks, for a fixed fee of USD 1,500. When property in Dubai is transferred, your heirs pay a registration fee to the Dubai Land Department: 0.125% of the property value, plus AED 1,000 per property.

The executor can do this **remotely**. They do not need to live in the UAE.

## Who can you choose?

In practice most owners choose:

- **their spouse**, who is often also the main heir. That is allowed: executor and beneficiary can be the same person;
- **an adult child**;
- **a sibling or close friend** they trust and who is well organised.

Choose someone you trust completely, who is willing to take on the task and who will remain reachable. Discuss it with them beforehand.

## Why a backup executor is wise

What if your executor has died, is ill or cannot do the job? Then the settlement gets stuck. That is why we recommend always naming **a backup executor**. It costs nothing extra and gives a lot of certainty.

A common set-up for couples: the spouse as executor, an adult child or a sibling as backup.

## Watch out with mirror wills

With mirror wills, both spouses register their own will, often with the same executor. Pay attention to the **relationship** you enter: your brother is "brother" to you, but "brother-in-law" to your spouse. The DIFC portal asks for the relationship per will.

## Executor or witness?

An executor may be a beneficiary. **Witnesses** follow different rules: they may not be a beneficiary, nor the partner of a beneficiary. So your spouse can be your executor, but not your witness.
