You own one apartment in the UAE. Perhaps in Dubai Marina, perhaps in Abu Dhabi or Ras Al Khaimah. You bought it as an investment, often through an agent, possibly without ever visiting it. And now you wonder: is it worth arranging a separate will for it? Or is that overkill for one property?

It is a fair question. And the answer depends less on what your apartment is worth than you might think.

## What the decision is really about

Most people weigh the cost of a will against the value of the apartment. That is the wrong comparison.

The right comparison is: what does it cost your heirs to inherit the apartment *with* a local will, versus *without* one?

Without a locally valid will, your heirs usually have to go through a full UAE inheritance procedure. That means legalising documents from your home country via the UAE embassy, arranging sworn Arabic translations and hiring local lawyers. The process often takes 12 months or longer and easily costs more than EUR 10,000. During that time the apartment often cannot be freely sold or rented, and linked bank accounts may be frozen.

With a DIFC Property Will, that procedure is largely unnecessary. You decide who inherits the apartment, and your heirs have a document the UAE authorities recognise.

## The value of the property hardly changes the sum

This surprises many owners: whether your apartment is worth EUR 150,000 or EUR 500,000, the procedure without a will is largely the same. The same legalisations, the same translations, the same lawyers, the same timeline.

In other words: for a less expensive apartment, a will is relatively *more* important, not less. The fixed cost of doing nothing weighs more heavily against the value.

## When it may be able to wait

We will not sell you a will you do not need. There are situations where it is less urgent:

- **You are about to sell the apartment.** If you sell soon and do not reinvest in the UAE, the need largely falls away.
- **The apartment is held in a structure with its own succession arrangement.** Then the situation is different and needs tailored work. Read [when a Property Will is not enough](/articles/when-is-a-property-will-enough/).

In almost every other case: as long as you own the apartment, the risk lies with your heirs, not with you. They only find out when you are no longer there to explain.

## A concrete example

A single owner with one villa in Abu Dhabi, bought as an investment. No children, a brother as the intended heir. A single Property Will is enough: he decides himself that the villa goes to his brother, and prevents the estate from getting stuck in an inheritance procedure, or, without identifiable heirs, from falling under the new Waqf rule.
